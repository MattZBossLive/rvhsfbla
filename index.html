About

What is FBLA?

FBLA stands for Future Business Leaders of America. It's the largest career and technical student organization for students interested in business, and there are chapters at high schools all over the country.

The part most people know about is competitive events. You choose an event in something like accounting or public speaking and prepare for it with the chapter. Then you compete against students from other schools. Chapters also do service projects and fundraisers during the year, and members can run for officer positions.

Riverview didn't have an FBLA chapter until this year. Mr. Green is our adviser, and since we're still new, the members who join now get a say in what we do and how we run things.

Why join

What you get out of it

Leadership

Officers run our meetings and plan what the chapter does each year. Elections happen every spring, and there's committee work you can pick up any time before that.

Scholarships

FBLA and its partner organizations offer scholarships for members. What's available changes each year, so check FBLA's website to see what's open.

College applications

Admissions offices know what FBLA is. If you place at a competition or lead a chapter project, you'll have something specific to talk about in essays and interviews.

Conferences

Competing means traveling to conferences and meeting students from other schools around Florida. If you qualify for state, that's a few days in Orlando with the chapter.

Competing

How competing works

Competing is optional. If you want to, you'll pick an event in the fall and prepare for it until the district conference in January. The top finishers there go on to the state conference, and the top finishers at state go to nationals.

Types of events

There are dozens of events, but every one of them uses one of these four formats.

100 questions, 50 minutes

Objective tests

A multiple choice test on one subject, such as accounting or cybersecurity. It's the easiest format to start with since all you have to do is study.

Test, then a case study

Hybrid events

It starts with an objective test. The second part is a case study, where you get a business problem and present your solution to a panel of judges.

Prepared or impromptu

Presentations

You present live in front of judges. For some events you build your presentation ahead of time, and for others you get the topic a few minutes before you speak.

Made before the conference

Submission events

You create something ahead of time, like a website or a business plan, and turn it in to be judged. Some of these add a presentation at the state and national levels.

Some events are for teams of three, and a few are only open to 9th and 10th graders.

When it happens

  1. Fall

    Pick your event

    Choose what you want to compete in and start preparing. You need to be a paid FBLA member by the registration deadline, so don't wait too long on dues.

  2. January

    District 9 Leadership Conference

    Objective tests are taken early in the month, and live events like presentations happen about a week later. Awards are announced near the end of January. The 2026 awards ceremony was held here in Riverview.

  3. March

    State Leadership Conference

    FBLA Florida's state conference is a few days long and held in Orlando. You compete against qualifiers from every district in the state.

  4. Summer

    National Leadership Conference

    Nationals are in a different city every year, usually at the end of June. The 2026 conference was in San Antonio.

We'll post this year's exact dates on our Instagram once FBLA Florida announces them.

Dues

$35 per year

You pay once for the whole school year. Most of it goes to your national and state FBLA memberships, and the rest stays with the chapter for supplies and competition prep.

Conference trips cost extra if you qualify and decide to go, and the chapter fundraises to help cover that. If the cost is a problem for you, talk to Mr. Green privately.

FAQ

Questions we get a lot

How do I join?

Message us on Instagram at @riverviewhighfbla or tell any officer. You can also stop by Mr. Green's room or just show up to a meeting. There's no application.

Do I need to know anything about business?

No. Most people start with no business background at all. The officers and Mr. Green can help you find an event that fits what you're already interested in.

How much time does it take?

Meetings are [FREQUENCY] and usually run less than an hour. If you compete, plan on some extra prep time in the few weeks before a conference.

Lots of members also play a sport or have a job. It's doable.

What does the $35 pay for?

Most of it covers your national and state FBLA memberships. The rest goes to the chapter for things like study materials. You only pay once a year, and conference trips are paid for separately.

Do I have to compete?

No. You can be a member without ever entering an event. If you're curious but nervous about it, team events are a good place to start because you'll have two teammates with you.

Can freshmen join?

Yes. Anyone in grades 9 through 12 can join. A few events are only open to 9th and 10th graders, so joining early gives you more options.

Will it help my college applications?

It can. Admissions officers know what FBLA is, and placing at a conference or leading a project gives you something specific to write about.

I don't want a business career. Should I still join?

Yes. There are events in coding, graphic design, journalism, public speaking, and plenty of other areas. Being able to present and interview well helps no matter what you end up doing.
