About

What is FBLA?

FBLA stands for Future Business Leaders of America. It's a national organization for students interested in business, with more than 200,000 members and chapters at high schools across the country.

Most of what members do is compete. You pick an event, like accounting, marketing, cybersecurity, or public speaking, prepare for it with the chapter, and take it on against students from other schools. Outside of competing, chapters run fundraisers and service projects, and members vote on officers every spring.

Riverview didn't have a chapter until this year. That means the students who join now have a say in which events we prepare for, how meetings run, and what we work on outside of competitions. Mr. Green is our faculty adviser.

Why join

What you get out of it

Leadership

Run for an officer seat or lead a committee. Officer elections happen every spring, and three seats are open right now.

Competition

Take a timed test, present to a panel of judges, or turn in a project to be scored. It's good practice for interviews and college coursework.

Scholarships

FBLA and its partner organizations offer scholarships to members. What's available changes each year, so check FBLA's website to see what's open.

College applications

Admissions offices know what FBLA is. Placing at a competition or leading a chapter project gives you something specific to write about in essays and interviews.

Conferences

Compete at the district conference in January and the state conference in Orlando in March. Top finishers at state move on to nationals in the summer.

People

Fundraisers, service projects, and practice sessions put you in a room with students from every grade. It's an easy way to meet people you wouldn't see in class.

Competing

How competing works

Competing is optional. If you want to, you'll pick an event in the fall and prepare for it until the district conference in January. The top finishers there go on to the state conference, and the top finishers at state go to nationals.

Types of events

There are dozens of events, but every one of them uses one of these four formats.

100 questions, 50 minutes

Objective tests

A multiple choice test on one subject, like accounting or cybersecurity. This is the easiest place to start, since all you have to do is study.

Test, then a case study

Hybrid events

You start with an objective test. The second part is a case study: a business problem you work through and present to a panel of judges.

Prepared or impromptu

Presentations

You present live in front of judges. For some events you build the presentation ahead of time, and for others you get the topic a few minutes before you speak.

Made before the conference

Submission events

You create something ahead of time, like a website or a business plan, and turn it in to be judged. Some of these add a presentation at the state and national levels.

Some events are for teams of up to three, and a few are only open to 9th and 10th graders.

When it happens

  1. Fall

    Pick your event

    Choose what you want to compete in and start preparing. You need to be a paid member by the registration deadline, so pay your dues early.

  2. January

    District 9 Leadership Conference

    Objective tests are taken early in the month, and live events like presentations happen about a week later. Awards are announced near the end of January.

  3. March

    State Leadership Conference

    FBLA Florida's state conference is held in Orlando over a long weekend. You compete against qualifiers from every district in the state.

  4. Summer

    National Leadership Conference

    Nationals are in a different city every year, usually around the end of June. The 2026 conference was in San Antonio.

We'll post this year's exact dates on our Instagram once FBLA Florida announces them.

FAQ

Questions we get a lot

How do I join?

Come to a C-day Shark School meeting, message us on Instagram at @riverviewhighfbla, or tell any officer. There's no application. Once you're in, you pay dues through MPP to be an official member.

When and where do you meet?

During Shark School on C days, in room 533. Shark School is the Monday period when students can go to a club instead of their homeroom, so on those days you can walk to room 533. If anything changes, we'll post it on Instagram.

How much are dues, and how do I pay?

Dues are $35 for the school year and are paid online through MyPaymentsPlus. Most of it covers your national and state FBLA memberships. The rest goes to the chapter for things like study materials. Conference trips are paid for separately.

Do I need to know anything about business?

No. Most people start with no business background at all. The officers and Mr. Green can help you find an event that fits what you're already interested in.

How much time does it take?

Regular meetings happen during Shark School, so they don't take up any after-school time. If you compete, plan on some extra prep time in the few weeks before a conference.

Do I have to compete?

No. You can be a member without ever entering an event. If you're curious but nervous about it, team events are a good place to start because you'll have teammates with you.

Can freshmen join?

Yes. Anyone in grades 9 through 12 can join. A few events are only open to 9th and 10th graders, so joining early gives you more options.

Can I become an officer?

Yes. The Secretary, PR and Social Media Manager, and Treasurer seats are open right now. Read about them in the open positions section and message us to apply.

Will it help my college applications?

It can. Admissions officers know what FBLA is, and placing at a conference or leading a project gives you something specific to write about.

I don't want a business career. Should I still join?

Yes. There are events in coding, graphic design, journalism, public speaking, and plenty of other areas. Being able to present and interview well helps no matter what you end up doing.
